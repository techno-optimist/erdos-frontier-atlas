The transfer argument

Choose a claw in the core. Give its center weight 1 and its three leaves weight 1/8; give every other core vertex a sufficiently small positive dyadic weight.

The weighted coefficients have f₂² − f₁f₃ ≤ −7/65536. If the weight at vertex v is 2−bᵥ, attach N+bᵥ pendant two-edge paths there.

With M equal to the total number of arms, aM+s / 2M−Ns → fs. The negative determinant therefore survives for every sufficiently large N. The core remains exactly the branching-vertex graph.

This is an informal proof, not a Lean formalization or a literature-priority claim. Nothing here proves the converse for path cores.
