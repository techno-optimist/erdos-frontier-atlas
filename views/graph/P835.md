## STATEMENT (Apache-licensed, Lean-derived)

### `S:triage:835` — triage verdict **WALL** (finite_handle_triage, 2026-07-26)

- our summary: Is there k>2 such that the k-subsets of {1,...,2k} can be coloured with k+1 colours so that every (k+1)-subset sees all k+1 colours among its k-subsets? Equivalently: is chi(J(2k,k)) = k+1 for some k>2, where J(2k,k) is the Johnson graph (k-sets adjacent iff they meet in k-1 elements)? Erdos-Rosenfeld [Er74d, p.283]; k+1 <= chi(J(2k,k)) <= 2k always.
- witness: CONCRETE. An explicit proper (k+1)-colouring of J(2k,k): a map from the C(2k,k) k-subsets of [2k] into {1,...,k+1} with no two sets meeting in exactly k-1 elements getting the same colour. Equivalently a partition of all k-subsets into k+1 constant-weight codes of length 2k, weight k, minimum Hamming distance 4. Storable as C(2k,k) bytes (184,756 bytes at k=10).
- smallest untested case: k = 10, i.e. decide whether chi(J(20,10)) = 11. Ma-Tang Thm 2.2 kills every k>2 with k+1 composite, so the only live k are k = p-1 for odd prime p: {4, 6, 10, 12, 16, 18, 22, ...}.
