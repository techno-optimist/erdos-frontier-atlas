- our summary: Are there finite sets of primes P,Q with (sum_{p in P} 1/p)(sum_{q in Q} 1/q) = 1?
- witness: Concrete: two disjoint finite lists of primes. Verification is one exact rational multiply — cheap and exact.
- smallest untested case: Not pinnable. The only hard constraint is |P union Q| >= 60 (from AM-GM: the product being 1 forces sum_{P union Q} 1/p >= 2, and reaching 2 needs the first ~59 primes). No upper bound on the largest prime is known, so there is no well-defined 'next case'.
- known frontier: None recorded. Upstream reports only Cambie's examples for the RELAXED (pairwise-coprime, not prime) version, e.g. 1 = (1+1/5)(1/2+1/3) and 1 = (1+1/41)(1/2+1/3+1/7); and that no example of even the relaxed version is known if 1 is excluded from P and Q. Asked by Barbeau [Ba76]; problem is from [ErGr80].
- search-space estimate: Capping at the first 100 primes (already an unjustified cap — nothing bounds the largest prime) gives 3^100 ~ 5e47 assignments of each prime to P / Q / neither, and any subset must have >= 60 elements. 35 orders of magnitude past budget.
- verification cheap and exact: yes
- verdict reason: Passes criteria 1 and 3 (concrete witness, exact cheap check) and fails 2 catastrophically. The search space is not merely large, it is UNBOUNDED — there is no known bound on the largest prime that could appear, so no finite enumeration is ever a complete search. The |P union Q| >= 60 floor guarantees the smallest possible witness already has 60+ primes, so even a bounded heuristic hunt starts at 3^60 ~ 4e28.
