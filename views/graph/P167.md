### `S:triage:167` — triage verdict **MAYBE** (finite_handle_triage, 2026-07-26)

- our summary: Tuza's conjecture: if G has at most k pairwise edge-disjoint triangles, can G be made triangle-free by deleting at most 2k edges? I.e. tau(G) <= 2*nu(G), where nu is the maximum edge-disjoint triangle packing and tau the minimum triangle edge-transversal. K4 and K5 show 2 is best possible.
- witness: CONCRETE, with a two-sided certificate. A graph G with tau(G) > 2*nu(G), shipped as an adjacency matrix plus (a) an explicit edge-disjoint triangle packing of size nu together with a proof of maximality, and (b) a proof that no edge set of size 2*nu hits every triangle. Both halves are pure-integer combinatorial optimisations — exact branch-and-bound, no floating point, no CAS. On <= 30 vertices this is seconds per candidate.
- smallest untested case: n = 9 is the first order that requires any enumeration at all: Puleo proved the conjecture whenever mad(G) < 7, and every graph on <= 7 vertices has mad <= 6, while on 8 vertices only K8 reaches mad = 7 (and K8 has tau = 12 <= 2*nu = 16). The first order where enumeration is nontrivial — and where I could find no published sweep — is n = 11 or n = 12.
- known frontier: No published exhaustive search over general graphs was found. The only exhaustive search located is Puleo (arXiv:1308.2211v3, European J. Combin. 2015, Section 3): a geng run with a custom pruning function for his forbidden configurations, restricted to genus <= 2, which "identified a set of only 5299 graphs that contains any smallest counterexample of genus 2", and he notes "for higher gamma, this computational approach quickly becomes intractible".
