### `S:triage:398` — triage verdict **WALL** (finite_handle_triage, 2026-07-26)

- our summary: Brocard-Ramanujan: are n = 4, 5, 7 the only solutions of n! = x^2 - 1?
- witness: Concrete in principle: an integer n > 7 with n!+1 a perfect square (equivalently the pair (n,x)). But at the frontier the object is not writable: n! for n ~ 10^15 has n*log10(n/e) ~ 1.4x10^16 decimal digits (~6 PB).
- smallest untested case: n = 10^15 + 1. NOTE: the upstream remark ("no other solutions below 10^9, see the OEIS page", page last edited 2026-04-01) is STALE — secondary sources (Wikipedia/HandWiki/Grokipedia, citing Matson and Epstein-Glickman) put the frontier at 10^15. Worth reporting upstream.
- known frontier: Berndt & Galway 2000: n < 10^9. Matson 2017 (unsolvedproblems.org S73, quadratic-residue method): n < 10^12. Epstein & Glickman 2020: n <= 10^15, optimised multi-core C++. Theory: Overholt 1993 — finitely many solutions under weak ABC; Naciri 2025 — finitely many if x±1 is k-free or a prime power, and n=4,5,7 only if x±1 is 7-free.
- search-space estimate: The QR sieve requires primes p > n (for n >= p, n! ≡ 0 mod p and n!+1 ≡ 1 is always a QR — no information), so each prime costs one modular multiply per n: pushing 10^15 -> 10^16 is ~10^16 modmuls per prime and ~50 primes to kill all survivors = ~5x10^17 modular multiplications. Not a one-workstation job by ~3 orders of magnitude.
- verification cheap and exact: no
- verdict reason: Fails criteria 2, 3 and 4. Refuting a candidate is cheap (QR test), but CONFIRMING a witness at n ~ 10^15 requires exact integer arithmetic on a 10^16-digit number — a 6-petabyte object, not a dependency-free certificate. The frontier is already at 10^15 with dedicated multi-core C++, and the heuristic count of solutions beyond n=7 is ~sum 1/sqrt(n!) ~ 0. Only realistic product is a frontier extension nobody can even verify cheaply.
- no move applies mechanically
