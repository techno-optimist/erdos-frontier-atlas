### `S:triage:7` — triage verdict **WALL** (finite_handle_triage, 2026-07-26)

- our summary: Is there a distinct covering system all of whose moduli are odd? A covering system is a finite set of congruences a_i mod n_i, all n_i distinct and > 1, such that every integer satisfies at least one. Asked by Erdos and Selfridge (sometimes with Schinzel).
- witness: NOT CONCRETE IN PRACTICE. Nominally a finite list of pairs (a_i, n_i) with distinct odd n_i > 1 covering Z. But nothing bounds the list length or the moduli, and every structurally comparable object we know of is astronomical: our own atlas boards #2/#27 record that a distinct covering system with minimum modulus 40-42 (Nielsen 2009, Owens 2014) already carries more than 10^50 congruences with an lcm period beyond enumeration. A witness is a recursively-defined construction, not a data file.
- smallest untested case: There is no size parameter to increment -- no 'n' to be at. The live constraints only narrow the shape: an odd covering must use a modulus divisible by 3 (Hough-Nielsen 2019: every distinct covering has a modulus divisible by 2 or 3), its lcm must be divisible by 9 or 15, and it cannot be squarefree (both Balister-Bollobas-Morris-Sahasrabudhe-Tiba 2022, killing the stronger Erdos-Selfridge question outright).
