## ATTACK SURFACES — which overlay said so

### `S:triage:672` — triage verdict **WALL** (finite_handle_triage, 2026-07-26)

- our summary: Can the product of an arithmetic progression n, n+d, …, n+(k−1)d of positive integers with k ≥ 4 and gcd(n,d)=1 be a perfect power?
- witness: A tuple (n, d, k, ℓ) with k ≥ 4, gcd(n,d)=1, ℓ ≥ 2 such that ∏_{t<k}(n+td) is an exact ℓ-th power. Verification is a product plus an exact integer ℓ-th root — cheap and exact.
- smallest untested case: k = 35 with ℓ = 2 or 3 (all ℓ are settled for 4 ≤ k ≤ 34; ℓ = 5 is separately settled for 3 ≤ k ≤ 54). n and d are unrestricted.
- known frontier: There is no computational frontier — the results are theorems, not searches. Euler (k=4, ℓ=2); Obláth [Ob51] for (k,ℓ)=(5,2),(3,3),(3,4),(3,5); Bennett–Bruin–Győry–Hajdu [BBGH06] for 4 ≤ k ≤ 11; Győry–Hajdu–Pintér [GHP09] for 4 ≤ k ≤ 34 and all ℓ; Bennett–Siksek [BeSi20] for all large k with prime ℓ > e^{10^k}; Marszalek [Ma85] gives k ≪_d 1 for fixed d.
- search-space estimate: UNBOUNDED — at k = 35 there is no bound on n or d, so there is no finite region to exhaust. For scale, even a blind sweep of n,d ≤ 10^6 is 10^12 coprime pairs × 35 multiplications on growing integers ≈ 3.5×10^13 ops and covers a measure-zero slice of the space.
- verification: True
- verdict reason: Criterion 2 fails absolutely: 'verifiable' here means only that if an example exists it is a finite tuple, not that it lives anywhere findable. Nothing bounds n or d, and Marszalek's k ≪_d 1 is the wrong direction (it bounds k given d, ineffectively for our purposes). The whole 50-year Baker/modularity line — Erdős–Selfridge, Obláth, BBGH, GHP, Bennett–Siksek — points at non-existence, so a search is hunting an object the theory says is not there in a space with no boundary. This is a Diophantine-theory problem wearing a finite-witness costume.
