### `S:triage:628` — triage verdict **WALL** (finite_handle_triage, 2026-07-26)

- our summary: Erdős–Lovász Tihany conjecture: if χ(G) = k and G has no K_k, and a,b ≥ 2 with a+b = k+1, then V(G) splits into two disjoint parts of chromatic number ≥ a and ≥ b.
- witness: A graph G with ω(G) < χ(G) = 6 that is not (2,5)-splittable. Usefully, non-(2,5)-splittability collapses by monotonicity to a finite local check: for EVERY edge uv, χ(G − u − v) ≤ 4. So the witness is G plus (i) a 6-colouring, (ii) a DRAT/UNSAT certificate that G has no 5-colouring, (iii) an explicit 4-colouring of G − u − v for each of the |E| edges. Concrete — but there is no bound of any kind on |V(G)|.
- smallest untested case: The pair (a,b) = (2,5), i.e. k = 6: a K_6-free graph with χ = 6 such that deleting the two endpoints of any edge drops χ to ≤ 4. Known pairs are exactly {(2,2),(2,3),(2,4),(3,3),(3,4),(3,5)} (confirmed in the introduction of arXiv:2607.20376), so (2,5) is the smallest open pair, with (4,4) at k=7 next. K_6-free 6-chromatic graphs exist as small as 10 vertices (C5 ∨ C5, ω=4, χ=6) — but that one IS (2,5)-splittable, and there is no theorem bounding where a counterexample could live.
- known frontier: None. There is no computational frontier at all — I found no computer search, no minimum-counterexample-order bound, and no enumeration in the literature. Progress is entirely structural: Brown–Jung [BrJu69] (2,3) and (3,3); Mozhan and Stiebitz independently (2,4); Stiebitz (3,4),(3,5); Balogh–Kostochka–Prince–Stiebitz [BKPS09] (quasi-line graphs, independence number 2); Song, arXiv:1805.11437 (graphs with forbidden holes); Longbrake–Tariq, arXiv:2406.15164 (claw-free, t ≤ 4s−3); Song, …[truncated; see source]

So an exhaustive sweep is arithmetically possible only to n ≈ 11 (n=12 already needs ~46 h of χ-computation alone, before generation).
