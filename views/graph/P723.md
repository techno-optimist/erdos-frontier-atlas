### `S:triage:723` — triage verdict **WALL** (finite_handle_triage, 2026-07-26)

- our summary: The prime power conjecture [Er81]: if a finite projective plane of order n exists must n be a prime power? A plane of order n is a family of (n+1)-subsets of {1,...,n^2+n+1} such that every pair of points lies in exactly one set.
- witness: CONCRETE. A projective plane of order n with n not a prime power. For n=12 that is a 157 x 157 zero-one incidence matrix with exactly 13 ones per row and per column and every point pair on exactly one line -- about 24,649 bits, roughly 3 KB.
- smallest untested case: n = 12. The conjecture is proved for n <= 11: n=6 by Bruck-Ryser [BrRy49] plus Tarry, n=10 by the Lam-Thiel-Swiercz computer search [La97]; n=14 is also excluded by Bruck-Ryser (14 = 2 mod 4 is not a sum of two squares). So n=12 is the unique open case below 14.
- known frontier: n=10 is the frontier and it was a landmark multi-year effort: Lam-Thiel-Swiercz 1989 consumed thousands of Cray-1 hours plus cluster time, and only in the last few years have pieces of it been independently re-verified with formal UNSAT certificates (Bright et al., arXiv:1911.04032 on weight-15 codewords; arXiv:2001.11974 on ovals).
